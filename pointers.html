Pointers to the Moon

A few spiritual books. All of these have spoken to me.

Autobiography of a Yogi by Paramahansa Yogananda. The classic story of a man’s devotion to his Guru, his search for God and his transformation into a Guru himself. Full of illumination, humour and pithy wisdom.

Miracle of Love by Sri Ram Dass. This book of spiritual satsangs on Shri Neem Karoli Baba is full of blessings, showing the multi-faceted nature of the devotional encounter, the invaluable grace of the Guru and the Guru’s words of wisdom. A book that inspires and takes you into the devotional fold no matter who you are and what creed you follow.

Ek Onkar Satnam (the True Name) by Osho. Shri Rajneesh’s incisive commentary on the Jap Sahib. The Hindi version is better than the English translation to my mind.

I Am That by Sri Nisargadatta Maharaj. A modern-day Upanishad. Incisive, clear, a superb summary of the Truth. It gets to the heart of the matter and eschews all paraphernalia.

Ashtavakra Mahagita (in Hindi, 9 volumes). The supreme message of freedom in the guise of the ancient dialogue between the supreme Guru, Rishi Ashtavakra, and his disciple Raja Janaka, the videhi (an epithet signifying transcendence of body-mind consciousness). This commentary by Shri Rajneesh, or Osho, in Hindi brings the clear spring of freedom out of the old frame in which it had been bottled. Aphoristic, biting and gives the courageous wings to soar. And alas, for those of us without knowledge of Sanskrit, the only path into this amazing treatise. A good translation in English is available at Click me.

The Heart of Awareness is a translation by Thomas Byrom; and there is another by Bart Marshall, which could be accessed via Google. This peerless scripture eludes elaboration. Before its verses, you lie defenceless. “Abandon desire, the enemy, along with gain, itself so full of loss, and the good deeds which are the cause of the other two — practice indifference to everything,” says Ashtavakra to Janaka, the spiritual aspirant. He speaks from the highest point of view, that of Absolute Truth, sheer Reality. Click me

Guru Vachaka Kovai by Murugnar (translated by Dr T. V. Venkatasubramanian, Robert Butler and David Godman). The Garland of Guru’s Sayings is an authoritative collection of Shri Ramana Maharishi’s sayings. An introduction and summation of how to live in the light of advaita (non-dual) teachings by the great saint of self-realisation.

Manache Shloke: Verses addressed to the Mind (translated by Swami Chidananda). A book compiled from the discourses the Swami gave on more than a forty couplets from this book written by the Guru of King Shivaji, Swami Samarth Ramdas. This book is full of practical advice: Chanting the Name of God be blessed in this world / Engage in good actions, daily worship, and discipline / Indifference to sense-objects is the essence of practice /Then, at all times, of thought-waves, you will be free.

God as Mother by Swami Chidananda is another superb book by the saint. It shows how the devotional mind looks at God as the divine mother, and goes behind the imagery of the Mother’s celebrated nine forms to show their inner appeal and essence. A must read. Click me

Sri Siddhi Ma: The Story of Neem Karoli Baba’s Spiritual Legacy by Jaya Prasada. An astounding account of bhakti, by a devoted servitor of the Mother of Kainchi, beloved Siddhi Ma, who herself was an exemplar of devotion to the great Sadguru, Shri Neem Karoli Maharaj.

The Gospel of Shri Ramakrishna (in two volumes). Sri M records his master’s words on every encounter he is privileged to participate in; for us, supreme guidance.

The Gospel of the Holy Mother. Full of spiritual pearls even as it recounts the daily life of the Mother, consort of Shri Ramakrishna, through the eyes of those who came for visitations to her.

Vivekananda: A biography by Swami Nikhilananda. Patriot, saint, jnani and bhakta, Vivekananda bought Hinduism to self-consciousness and to the consciousness of the world.

Any of Swami Vivekananda’s lectures on Karma, Bhakti, Gyana and Raja Yoga, compiled as separate booklets.

God Alone: The life and letters of a Saint by Sri Gyanmata. A snapshot of a perfected disciple’s devotion towards her Guru, Swami Paramhansa Yogananda.

Aghora by Robert Svoboda. An incredible trilogy on the worship of the Divine Mother. Unveils the devotional pulse that gives life to scarce-understood rituals and belittled rites even as it plunges into the worship of the Mother, which is the foundational ideal of dharma in Bharata. Not for the immature.

Devi: The Devi Bhagvatam Retold by Ramesh Menon. A translation of the Devi Bhagvat.

Ramayana by William Buck. A superb retelling in English that brings out the spirit of the original by Rishi Valmiki.

The Guru Gita by Swami Narayanananda . The Guru Gita is in three chapters and is chanted as a dialogue between Shri Uma and Shri Mahesvara. One signal verse shows that Guru, Shiva, and Shakti are identical, with the white-and-red hues of the Guru’s elegant feet commingling the description of Shiva as karpurgaurum (as white as camphor) and Devi as raktavarna (incarnadine). The essence (tattva) is one and the same, but unified in the august feet of the Guru.

My Guru and His Disciple by Christopher Isherwood. A sincere and honest account of the famous writer’s more than 30-year journey with his guru, Swami Prabhavananda, and through the world. Refreshingly non-hagiographic.

The Upanishads: Breath of the Eternal translated by Swami Prabhavananda and Frederick Manchester. The Swami was of the Ramakrishna Order, and this book is a compendium from the 12 Upanishads. A great book to start with..

Indian Saints and Mystics by Pravrajika Suddhaatmaprana. The life stories of 27 saints; invaluable inspiration.

The First and Last Freedom by Jiddu Krishnamurti. Krishna-ji comes to you as straight as a bullet, without rhetoric, without religious trappings, without any metaphysical baggage. Perhaps he is the rebellious seeker’s finest companion, and yet again a mystery and mystic. His writings are available at Click me.

Swami Sivananda and the Divine Life Society. A number of books are available from this spiritual treasure house. Not only books by the great guru, but those by Swami Chidananda and Swami Krishnananda are invaluable. A suggested reading list is given here. Many books are available for online reading for free.

Old Path, White Clouds: Walking in the footsteps of the Buddha by Thich Nhat Hanh. A heartfelt ode to and biography of the Buddha drawing from innumerable Pali sources. Also available in Hindi as Jahan Jahan Charan Pare Gautam Ke.

The miracle of mindfulness by Thich Nhat Hanh. An uncomplicated treatise on the supreme path of moment-to-moment awareness by the Vietnamese master.

The Way of the Bodhisattva by Shantideva (the Padmakara Translation Group). The book that is a favourite of the Dalai Lama, with commentary.

Healing Anger: The power of patience from a Buddhist perspective by the Dalia Lama.

Everyday Wisdom by Dr Wayne W. Dyer. A booklet of inspirational thoughts.

Only Love: Living the spiritual life in a changing world by Sri Daya Mata. A fountainhead of guidance and illumination by Sri Paramahansa Yogananda’s foremost disciple and successor.

What is Man: Selected writings of Sri Madhava Ashish. The disciple of Yoga Sri Krishnaprem, the Western devotee who made the Himalayan foothills his home and Vaishnavism his path to the divine.

Books on Guruji.

Light of Divinity. A compilation of devotees’ life-transforming narratives compiled at Guruji’s behest.

Surcharged with divine love by Sukhmani. A disciple’s thoughtful and ardent presentation of Guruji, showing how the path of love is forged. Vividly notes the master’s teachings in His own words. Invaluable.

My Guruji’s Grace by Dr M. M. Bajaj. Embodies the basic tenets of devotion.

Blessings Always by Rajkumar Sharma.
